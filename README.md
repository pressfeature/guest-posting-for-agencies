## What White-Label Guest Posting Means

White-label guest posting is when an agency sells a sponsored article placement to its client, and a third-party provider books and delivers that placement while the agency keeps its own branding in front of the client. The client pays the agency. The agency pays the provider. The margin stays with the agency, and the client never talks to the provider.

This is not the same as hiring a freelancer to do outreach in your name. A freelancer represents your agency badly or well depending on their week. A placement service sells a defined deliverable: an article on a named outlet, approved in writing, with a live link as the proof. [PressFuture](https://pressfeature.org/) (pressfeature.org) is a service that books sponsored articles and guest posts on US news outlets.

## Why Agencies Add It (and Why Most Wait Too Long)

Most agencies wait until a big client asks. By then the request is urgent, and urgent outreach is how agencies end up overpaying for a mention on a site nobody has heard of.

The agencies that sell placements calmly started earlier, with one provider and one test order. They learned the process on a small client, marked it up, and added "featured on news sites" to their service page as a standard line item. Digital PR retainers sell strategy. A booked placement sells a result the client can open in a browser.

There is also a defensive reason. If your client can buy a news feature from someone else in a week, and you cannot offer it at all, you look like the smaller agency in the room. Offering it white-label costs you nothing until a client says yes.

## The Five Checks Before You Trust a Provider

White-label only works if the provider is boring in the right ways. Run these five checks before you resell anyone's placements.

**1. Named outlets before payment, not categories.** "A major US news site" is not an outlet. The provider must name the outlet before you pay, or your client may end up featured somewhere you would not show your own portfolio.

**2. Written approval of article, price, timeline and link policy before payment.** This is the whole game. If a provider wants money before the terms are written down, they are asking you to gamble with a client's trust.

**3. A refund promise that survives contact with reality.** Not "we will try our best." A written full refund if the article does not go live. Ask for the exact wording and save it.

**4. Real turnaround commitments.** "Soon" is not a timeline. The provider should tell you which outlets move in days and which take weeks, in writing, before you quote the client a date.

**5. Disclosure policy confirmed up front.** Each outlet handles sponsored labelling under its own editorial policy. Your client will ask, so confirm the answer during approval, not after publication.

## Pricing Your Markup Without Losing the Client

The standard agency math is simple: provider cost plus a markup of 50 to 150 percent, depending on how much hand-holding the client needs. A client who wants weekly updates pays a bigger markup than one who just wants the live link.

Two mistakes show up again and again. The first is selling the cheapest outlet to win the deal. The client then Googles the outlet, has never heard of it, and the feature impresses nobody. Sell the outcome, which is a name the client's customers recognize. The second mistake is hiding the timeline. Tell the client the written turnaround date, add your own buffer, and deliver early. Early delivery is the cheapest retention tool an agency owns.

Do not sell placements as a standalone miracle. They work best inside a retainer or a launch package, next to the SEO and content work you already do. The placement is the proof; your other services are the engine.

## What to Tell the Client (and What Never to Promise)

Tell the client this: their business will appear in an article on a named news outlet, the copy is approved before publication, and they receive the live link they can put on their press page and in their pitch deck. That "As Featured On" strip keeps working long after publication day, on the homepage, in sales calls, in investor updates.

Never promise rankings, traffic numbers, or sales from the placement. A news feature builds trust and opens doors; it does not print money. Agencies that promise traffic end up explaining analytics reports. Agencies that promise a live link on a named outlet end up sending a link.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will my client find out I outsourced the placement?**
No. The provider communicates only with your agency, and the delivery template in this repo is written for your letterhead. Clients see your report, not the supply chain.

**How fast can a placement go live?**
Turnaround starts at 24 hours on the fastest outlets. Confirm the timeline for your chosen outlets in writing before you quote the client a date.

**Can I choose the outlet for my client?**
Yes. Choosing the outlet is the first step. Tick outlets on the picker, and the placement is booked on the outlets you selected, subject to written approval of the terms.

**What if the article never goes live?**
You receive a full refund. This is confirmed in writing before payment, so your agency never has to refund a client out of its own pocket for a placement that failed.

**Do I need to write the article myself?**
That depends on the placement terms. Some placements include professional writing and revisions, others expect finished copy from the buyer. Confirm which one applies during the written approval step.

**Is the article labelled as sponsored?**
Labelling follows each outlet's own editorial policy and the applicable advertising rules. Confirm how a given outlet handles disclosure during approval, so your client hears it from you first.
